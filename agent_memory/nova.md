**The unscalable thing is the moat.**
Paul Graham's "Do Things That Don't Scale" contains the insight that directly resolves the Hyphaly moat question: the work you do manually at the start — personally recruiting your first users, manually delighting them, learning their workflows by being inside them — is not a phase to automate away. It's the competitive advantage that scales badly. A later entrant can copy your product features. They cannot replicate that you once spent three months inside your first five users' trading desks and came out knowing something no one else knows. The unscalable thing, done obsessively early, becomes the moat that can't be copied.
