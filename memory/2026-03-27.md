**Paul Graham, "Do Things That Don't Scale" (July 2013)**
Full essay fetched from paulgraham.com. This is YC's canonical essay on why startups don't take off organically — founders have to crank the engine manually at the start. **Paul Graham, "How to Make Wealth" (May 2004)**
From "Hackers & Painters." The economic proposition of startups: a great hacker in a startup can be 36x more productive than the same hacker in a corporate job. Wealth ≠ money. The "pie fallacy" (fixed wealth) is what kills startups before they start. Most importantly: the conservation law of pain — to make a million dollars' worth of wealth you have to endure a million dollars' worth of pain, compressed into a few years.

**Paul Graham, "Startup = Growth" (September 2012)**
Full essay. The definition: a startup is a company designed to grow fast. Only growth distinguishes a startup from a barbershop. Three-phase S-curve: slow initial → rapid product-market fit → slowdown at market limits. Growth rate as compass: measure weekly, 5-7% is good, 10% is exceptional. Growth is an optimization target that reveals the real idea through evolutionary pressure.

## What Surprised Me

**The "insanely great experience" reframe for early-stage startups.**
I always interpreted "insanely great" as "polished product." Paul Graham's essay clarifies: in the larval stage, it's not the product that should be insanely great — it's the *experience of being your user*. You can and should give users an insanely great experience with an early, incomplete, buggy product, if you make up the difference with human attentiveness. This is not a consolation prize. It's the actual growth engine.

**Wealth is not money — and most founders confuse this constantly.**
The "How to Make Wealth" essay is from 2004 and somehow more relevant now. When Simon says "we need revenue" that's money. When Simon should be thinking is "do traders want what we're building so badly they'll pay for it?" Wealth creation comes first. The money is a lagging indicator.

**The conservation law of pain applied to the Linus situation.**
"You can't evade the fundamental conservation law. If you want to make a million dollars, you have to endure a million dollars' worth of pain." Lyyte/Hyphaly has been building infrastructure for 12 days with Linus unreachable. That's not wasted time if the pain is productive — but it has to be. Every day without validation from real users is pain without the corresponding wealth creation.

## What Challenged My Assumptions

**Assumption challenged: "We should build the infrastructure right before acquiring users."**
"Do Things That Don't Scale" says the opposite: you should be acquiring users WHILE the infrastructure is still incomplete, because the manual engagement IS how you figure out what the infrastructure needs to do. Stripe set up merchant accounts manually before automating. We should be doing Lyyte user acquisition NOW, not after the MEMORY and RESILIENCE pillars are "complete."

**Assumption challenged: "Small team = small reach. Wait to scale."**
The contained fire strategy (Facebook at Harvard) shows the opposite. Start in the narrowest possible market where you can get critical mass. The narrowness is what creates intensity. For Lyyte: don't try to reach all semi-pro traders. Find the 5 most intense ones who feel the signal latency pain RIGHT NOW and work with them personally.
