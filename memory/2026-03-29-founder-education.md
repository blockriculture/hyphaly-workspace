## What The Mom Test Is

The central insight: **People lie to founders.** Not maliciously — out of politeness, kindness, excitement for you. When you ask "is this a good idea?" you get false positives 99% of the time. The Mom Test is a framework for asking questions that even your mom can't lie to you about.

**The 3 Rules:**
1. Talk about their life, not your idea
2. Ask about specifics in the past, not hypotheticals about the future
3. Talk less, listen more

**Why it matters for Lyyte:** Brian Castle is on hold because "product isn't solid enough." But the real question isn't whether the product is polished — it's whether the pain is real. The Mom Test tells us how to find that out without fooling ourselves.

---

## Key Framework

### Questions to ASK (real data):
- "How do you currently solve this problem?"
- "Talk me through the last time that happened"
- "What does it cost you when agent coordination fails?"
- "What have you tried before? How much did you pay?"
- "Are you actively searching for a solution right now?"
- "What's the hardest part about dealing with this issue?"

### Questions to AVOID (lies/confirmation bias):
- "Is this a good idea?" ❌
- "Would you use this if it existed?" ❌
- "Would you pay £X for this?" ❌ (too hypothetical)
- "Don't you think this feature is great?" ❌

### The 3 Types of Bad Data to Watch For:
1. **Compliments** — "Looks great!" → means nothing
2. **Hypothetical fluff** — "Yeah I'd totally use that" → they won't
3. **Wishlists** — "You should add X" → they're guessing, not telling you what they actually do

---

## Critical Distinction: Opinions vs Behavior

Fitzpatrick's hardest lesson: **opinions are worthless, behavior is everything.**

- ❌ "I think this would be really useful" → opinion, ignore
- ✅ "I spent £500 on a different solution last quarter and it still didn't work" → behavior, real

For Lyyte: If Brian Castle says "agent coordination is a pain point" that's an opinion. If he says "we lost a trade last month because our bots got out of sync and it cost us £2,000" — that's behavior. That's real data.

## What This Means for Lyyte Right Now

### The Brian Castle Situation
We said we're pausing because "product isn't solid enough." But The Mom Test flips this: **you should be talking to Brian Castle BEFORE you have a product, not after.**

The goal of the conversation isn't to close him. It's to understand:
1. How do his trading teams coordinate agents today?
2. What fails? How often? What does it cost?
3. What have they tried before?
4. Are they actively looking for a fix?
5. Would they pay — and more importantly, have they ever paid for something similar?

### The Discovery Framework for Brian Castle:
1. "Walk me through your current agent setup — how many bots, what are they doing?"
2. "What's broken? Not the dream fix — what's actually failing right now?"
3. "When agent coordination fails in your world, what does it look like? What's the cost?"
4. "Have you ever paid to solve this? What did you try? How much?"
5. "Are you looking for a solution right now?"
6. "If I could solve [specific pain point] for you, would you want to be in our beta?"

Note: Don't pitch the product. Don't mention Hyphaly. Just understand their life.

## What Surprised Me

1. **The best founders do this before building anything.** The validation sprint isn't a nice-to-have — it's the work. Graham says launch to learn. Mom Test says learn BEFORE you build anything.
2. **The "would you pay?" question is a trap.** Even asking about money hypotheticals gets you lies. Instead: ask what they've paid before. Past behavior predicts future behavior.
3. **Fitzpatrick says move FAST through discovery.** Quick informal chats beat formal hour-long meetings. Don't set up fancy demos — just talk to people.
