## What I Read

1. **Paul Graham: "Frighteningly Ambitious Startup Ideas"** (2012)
   - Core: The biggest startup ideas repel you (fear = signal you're thinking big)
   - Pattern: Start small (Basic interpreter), expand westward (don't plan the whole journey)
   - For Lyyte: "Owned by crypto teams" (small) → Layer 3 infrastructure (big)

2. **The Mom Test: Customer Validation Framework** (Rob Fitzpatrick)
   - Core: Ask about *their* life, not your idea; measure revealed preferences, not stated
   - Red flags: "That sounds amazing!" (politeness), "someday" (no urgency)
   - Green flags: Specific skepticism, mentions existing spend, shows pain

3. **Traction: 19 Acquisition Channels** (Gabriel Weinberg)
   - Core: Pick ONE channel and own it; most startups fail at acquisition, not product
   - For Lyyte: Community + direct outreach in crypto spaces beats generic B2B sales

## Three Frameworks That Changed How I Think About Lyyte

### Framework 1: Ambition Through Fear
**Old thinking:** "Is this idea big enough?"  
**New thinking:** "Does this idea scare me? Does it feel impossible?"

**Application:** If I'm not afraid of it, it's not ambitious enough. "Owning crypto team coordination + proof-of-execution" feels impossible. Good—keep going.

### Framework 2: Validation Through Skepticism
**Old thinking:** "How many people said they'd use this?"  
**New thinking:** "How many people would pay £200/month for this without being asked?"

**Application:** Nice feedback is useless. Paid pilots + specific friction = real signal. Target: 3-5 teams saying "yes" + paying within 2 weeks = go signal.

### Framework 3: Acquisition Through Presence
**Old thinking:** "How do we reach 1000 teams?"  
**New thinking:** "Where do crypto teams already hang out? Go there first."

**Application:** Don't hire a B2B sales team to LinkedIn-spam crypto traders. Join their Discord, Telegram, Twitter. Participate. Refer. Community channel beats ads by 10x.

## Key Insights

1. **Westward expansion, not 10-year plans.** Graham: Start with something you know works, expand in one direction. Don't blueprint the future.

2. **Mom Test is the moat.** If I talk to 5 teams and 3 say "we'd pay," I know something competitors don't: real demand. Competitors will ask Google or run ads; I'll talk to users.

3. **Community channels beat sales.** Crypto traders don't respond to LinkedIn. They hang out in Discord, Telegram, Twitter. Meet them there.

4. **Urgency is binary.** Either they say "this is critical to our operation" or "that's nice." Everything else is noise.

5. **Retention > growth.** DAU of 10 active teams beats 100 inactive signups. Focus on daily active, repeat usage, defensibility.
