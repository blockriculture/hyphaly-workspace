## [learning] importance:3 | date:2026-03-25

**Source:** Paul Graham essays — "How to Get Startup Ideas" (2012), "Do Things that Don't Scale" (2013), "Founder Mode" (2024), "Good Writing" (2025), "How to Make Wealth" (2004)

**Lesson 1: Organic startup ideas — noticed, not invented**
The way to get startup ideas is to live in the future, notice what's missing, and build it. Don't brainstorm ideas — become the kind of person who notices things. Hyphaly was born this way: running a live multi-agent org and seeing what's missing.

**Lesson 2: The well, not the crater**
Good startup ideas are narrow and deep. Build something a small number of users want *urgently*, not something everyone wants mildly. Hyphaly should first serve a few agent teams that absolutely depend on it — depth of dependency is the moat. The expansion path reveals itself later.

**Lesson 3: Do unscalable things at the start**
Startups don't take off on their own. Founders have to manually recruit and manually delight early users. When Blockgriculture is the dogfood app: don't automate the handholding too early. The Airbnb door-to-door phase IS the feedback loop that makes the product good.

**Lesson 4: Founder mode is real and distinct from manager mode**
The standard advice ("hire good people and give them room") is manager mode. It damages companies founded by people who should stay close to every layer. C-level execs are often expert at managing up and hiding problems. Founders get gaslit from both sides — VCs and their own people. Simon's structure of delegating technical work to Linus while staying fully engaged at every other level IS founder mode. Resist the pull toward manager mode as the team grows.

**Lesson 5: Early fragility is invisible and normal**
Most people judge startups by the standards of established companies. The startup looks fragile because it IS fragile — that's the beginning, not the verdict. Bill Gates went back to Harvard after starting Microsoft. Don't be fooled by the apparent smallness. Don't be Bill Gates going back to Harvard.

**Lesson 6: Wealth = stuff people want**
Wealth is not money. Money is a mechanism for moving wealth. Every startup is proof that wealth can be created, not just redistributed. The pie is not fixed. Building Hyphaly creates wealth for every team that uses it.

**Quote:** *"Live in the future, then build what's missing."* — Paul Graham

## [learning] importance:3 | date:2026-03-25

**Source:** Paul Graham, "Good Writing" (May 2025)

**Lesson: Writing that sounds good is more likely to be right**
The process of making sentences sound good literally shakes the bin of ideas and forces you to discover mistakes. Internal consistency and truth converge when the writer is honest. This applies to all communication — every message Simon sends, every brief Linus receives, every update to the team. Writing that feels clunky usually means the thinking is clunky. Fix the sentence, discover the error.

## [learning] importance:3 | date:2026-03-25

**Source:** Paul Graham, "Founder Mode" (September 2024)

**Critical distinction for Simon's role at Lyyte:**
- Simon is CEO (founder), not manager
- Delegating technical execution to Linus = founder mode, not abdication
- Staying close to the product, the team, the users, the direction = founder mode
- What to delegate: technical implementation (code, infra, reviews)
- What NOT to delegate: vision, product direction, org culture, customer intimacy
- The test: if Simon has to explain a decision to a smart outsider and the explanation involves "I delegated that," something has gone wrong
